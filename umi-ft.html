UMI-F/T

Wireless Force-Sensing Hardware Extension for the Universal Manipulation Interface

Making physical interaction measurable.

I designed and prototyped a wireless two-axis force-sensing hardware extension for the Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI), focusing on mechanical integration, fabrication, and preliminary functional verification.

Overview & Research Objective

Adding a sense
of force.

Accessible, low-cost demonstration collection tools are important for building the datasets used in robot learning. The original Universal Manipulation Interface, developed by Shuran Song’s research group, provides a practical platform for collecting visual observations and manipulation demonstrations.

Our team explored extending this platform with physical interaction measurements. My focus was a wireless module for measuring X–Y loads at the gripper’s wrist-mounting interface.

The objective was to support future synchronized vision-and-force data collection, force-aware robot learning, and contact-rich manipulation. In this project, our work focused on hardware development and preliminary functional verification.
