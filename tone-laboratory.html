My Tone Lab - PedalBoard

This is my main guitar pedalboard, which I have built over the years while exploring various guitar tones and audio effects.

By combining analog pedals with digital effects, it offers a wide range of sounds and flexible control for live performances and recording.

SIGNAL INTEGRITY & ROUTING

I carefully designed the cable lengths and signal routing between effects to minimize potential signal degradation in the long signal chains.
I also configured the buffers and routing with consideration for signal impedance and cable capacitance, while maintaining the flexibility of the system.

Effects Signal Chain - Block Diagram

Effects Signal Chain

Input → Kill switch → Tuner/Buffer → Feedbacker → Compressor

→ Modulation (Pre) → Noise Reduction (Send) → MIDI Controller (Drive Station) → Noise Reduction (Return) → Amp/Cab Sim

→ Modulation (Post) → Delay → Reverb → Output

The diagram illustrates the overall signal flow of my pedalboard. The guitar signal first passes through a passive kill switch and an always-buffered tuner to maintain signal integrity. A feedback pedal is placed before downstream processing, followed by a compressor to shape the signal's dynamics.

SIGNAL ROUTING DETAILS

  1. Modulation Pre / Post mode.

    The Strymon Mobius uses its stereo inputs and outputs to support flexible Pre/Post routing, allowing modulation effects to be placed before or after the gain stage. Only one position can be active at a time.

  2. Noise reduction G string loop.

    Conventional noise reduction can abruptly cut off sustain. To address this, the G-String loop isolates the high-gain section while using the clean guitar signal as a detection reference. This reduces noise while preserving natural sustain and decay.

  3. MIDI / Control programmable architecture.

    To avoid excessive pedal switching during performances, I use a MIDI controller with seven analog loops for drive pedals. It also controls three digital processors through MIDI commands and tempo synchronization. A single preset can recall multiple drive combinations, digital effects settings, and tempo, allowing the entire system to function like a programmable multi-effects processor.

  4. Optional amp / cab stage.

    An amp simulator is placed after the drive section and before the spatial effects. For home recording or direct PA use, I typically use a Fender Deluxe Reverb-style clean amp simulation. When using a physical tube amplifier, I bypass this stage.

  5. Post spatial chain.

    After modulation, delay and reverb shape the temporal and spatial characteristics of the sound. The dual-engine architecture of the Strymon MX series supports serial, parallel, and split configurations before the signal reaches the output.

03

System Components

Paul Cochrane Tim V1

This is a transparent, low-gain overdrive with wide headroom and a warm, rounded sound.

I mainly use it as my foundation tone or as a standalone boost.

Compared to the Jan Ray, it offers more headroom and a slightly rawer character.

Analogman King of Tone V4

Both Side Normal Gain

I set the Yellow side to Clean mode for sharp, percussive rhythm playing.
The Red side is set to OD mode, providing a tighter and thicker overdrive than the Tim.

By stacking both sides, I can get a distortion-level gain suitable for guitar solos.

Fulltone OCD V1.2

The OCD V1.2 is one of the most sought-after versions of the pedal, offering a dark, creamy sound that sits between overdrive and distortion.

I mainly use it with low gain and tone settings.

I often stack it with King of Tone's Clean side, combining smooth saturation with a tight midrange to create a balanced lead tone.

ProCo RAT Whiteface 1984

This vintage RAT offers a wide gain range, from overdrive to fuzz, with the natural, wild character of its LM308N chip.

At its sweet spot, crispy harmonics add to its rich saturation.

I mainly use it on its own for clear, technical lead tones in J-Pop and City Pop.

Electro HarmonixBig Muff V3 1976

This Big Muff delivers a powerful distortion and fuzz sound, using a four-transistor circuit with two clipping stages based on the Violet Ram's Head design.

Its thick saturation and long sustain make it ideal for heavy metal rhythm and lead tones.

I mainly use it on its own for these sounds.

Revv G3

This is a modern high-gain distortion pedal with an extremely tight, controlled, and aggressive sound.

However, its sharp character can sometimes make the tone feel quite thin.
I usually stack it with the MESA Throttle Box to add more body and fullness.

Its high-gain settings also produce significant noise, making a noise gate necessary.

MESA Throttle Box

The Throttle Box is a heavy metal distortion pedal with an extremely dark and heavy sound.

At maximum gain, it can also produce fuzz-like tones.

However, its heavy character can sometimes feel too muddy or sluggish. I mainly use it in low-gain mode, stacking it with the Revv G3 to add character while maintaining tightness, creating a refined metal lead tone.

Origin EffectsCali76 Compact Deluxe

The Cali76 Compact Deluxe is a studio-grade FET compressor with a distinctive warm coloration.

I mainly use it with light compression at a 4:1 ratio to control excessive transients.
For percussive rhythm playing, I use stronger compression with fast attack and release settings to get a tight, punchy feel.

I bypass it when I want to preserve the full dynamics of my playing.

ISP DECI-MATE Micro G

The Deci-Mate Micro G is a high-quality noise reduction pedal that uses direct guitar signal tracking.

Its loop allows the drive section to be gated independently.

I mainly use it with the Revv G3 + Throttle Box combination and the Big Muff to suppress unwanted noise while preserving fast attack and long sustain.

DigiTech Freqout

This is a feedback generator that produces controlled feedback without an amplifier.

I mainly use it for live performances, creating smooth, violin-like lead textures through harmonic sustain.

MusicomLab EFX-LEⅡ

I assign all seven analog loops to drive pedals and use the controller to manage my Strymon MIDI processors.

It allows me to control effect on/off states, signal routing order, and tap tempo through presets, making the entire signal chain programmable.

IK Multimedia TONEX One

I mainly use this as a Fender Deluxe Reverb-style clean amp platform.

When performing live with a physical amplifier, I bypass it.

For home recording or direct PA setups, I use it as an amp/cab simulation stage.

Strymon Mobius

The Mobius supports Pre/Post routing, allowing me to place modulation effects before or after the drive/amp stage.

For example, I use a flanger in Pre mode to create a rougher, more blended sound by running it through the drive section.

In post mode, I use rotary and chorus effects to add subtle movement and color after distortion.

Strymon Timeline MX

The Timeline MX is an advanced delay processor that uses high-resolution processing to create not only repeats but also various textures and sonic accents.

I usually use Digital Delay for fast, clear repeats, while Oil Can provides a more analog, reverb-like texture. Combined with the Bigsky MX, it helps create a cohesive ambient environment.

Strymon Bigsky MX

The Bigsky MX is an advanced reverb processor that uses high-resolution processing to create an environment for my playing rather than simply adding an effect.

I mainly use Room reverb as my default setting.
When needed, I create expansive spatial textures that shape the atmosphere and sense of scale of a song.

TC ElectronicPolytune3 mini noir

The PolyTune 3 Mini Noir is a compact, high-performance tuner with an always-on input buffer.

Its high-quality buffered bypass helps minimize signal degradation in long signal chains.

I mainly use it to maintain signal integrity rather than for tuning.

JHS Stutter Switch

This is a passive momentary kill switch that briefly cuts the guitar signal when pressed.

I mainly use it during live performances to create rhythmic stutter effects through rapid signal cuts.

Fulltone Clyde Deluxe

This is an expressive wah pedal with a wide sweep range.

I mainly use Jimi mode for percussive, rhythmic playing.
In Shaft mode, I use slower sweeps to create deeper, wider filter movements.

Strymon Zuma / Ojai

They are high-quality power supplies with fully isolated outputs, helping maintain a very low noise floor.

They provide clean, consistent power to all my pedals, ensuring stable operation of both digital processors and sensitive vintage analog pedals.

My Guitars

Each guitar has its own tonal character and playing characteristics. I choose my instruments based not only on the genre of a song, but also on the tone, playing style, and musical expression I want to achieve.

Since pickups, electronics, and physical characteristics also play important roles in shaping the sound, I use each guitar as a different sound design tool.

Gibson Custom1963 Les Paul SG Custom Reissue, VOS

This guitar features a warm, full-bodied vintage sound from its PAF humbuckers.

Unlike a typical SG, it produces a tone closer to a Les Paul, while offering a lighter, thinner body and a more open, powerful drive sound.

I especially love the clear, glassy character of its middle pickup. Reminiscent of the in-between pickup positions on a Stratocaster, I often use this sound for Neo-Funk playing.

I mainly use this guitar for Blues and Classic Rock, where it serves a similar role to a Les Paul.

It is one of my most cherished guitars.

FenderAmerican Vintage Reissue '65 Stratocaster

This guitar was passed down to me by my guitar teacher and features natural relic wear from years of playing.

It is one of the guitars I feel most comfortable picking up, even without a specific purpose. I use it across a wide range of genres, from casual indie music to jazz and blues.

Equipped with Fender Custom Shop Fat '50s pickups, it offers a slightly thicker and fuller low end than a typical Stratocaster.

I mainly prefer the smooth, warm sound of the neck pickup. For funky rhythm playing, I use the bright, glassy sound of the in-between pickup positions.

When using light RAT-style distortion, I switch to the bridge pickup for a sharper, more defined drive tone.

Its versatility across different musical situations makes it one of my most frequently used guitars.

Ibanez J.CustomRG8570 BRE

This is my technical guitar, built for fast and aggressive playing.

I mainly use it for Modern Rock, Hard Rock, and Metal, where it excels in fast phrases and precise techniques.

Its low string action makes fast fingering and tapping easier, allowing me to play complex passages with greater ease.

Whenever a song demands extreme speed or an aggressive playing style, I reach for this guitar first.

In short, this is my battle guitar.

Fender JapanTraditional '60s Jazzmaster

I mainly use this guitar for City Pop and Ambient Music.

Although the Jazzmaster may seem somewhat niche, its tonal character is actually quite common in J-Pop.

I replaced the original pickups with Pure Vintage '65 pickups to get a softer, more acoustic-like tone.

The Jazzmaster's unique rhythm circuit allows me to switch between different tones almost like presets.

By setting the rhythm circuit's volume to zero, I can also use its switch as a makeshift kill switch.

However, the bridge and in-between pickup positions can sound a bit too sharp in the high frequencies.

To address this, I usually roll the tone knob down to around 4-5 for a smoother, more balanced sound.

I especially enjoy pairing it with chorus effects, making it one of my favorite choices for spacious ambient sounds and bright, casual J-Pop playing.

Another Tone Lab - Bass PedalBoard

ABOUT THE RIG

This pedalboard is my analog recreation of the BDDI, 1176, LA-2A, and EQP-1A signal chain that I often use for bass recording.

MY SOUND PHILOSOPHY

I prefer a rich, full-bodied bass tone over heavily processed or distorted, synth-like sounds.

My goal is to create a bass sound that naturally connects the drums with other instruments and supports the music as a whole.

To achieve this, I arranged a preamp, two different types of compressors, and an equalizer in sequence.

Effects Signal Chain

Input → Preamp → 1176-Style FET Comp → Optical Comp → EQ → Output

System Components

Tech21SansAmp Bass Drive DI V1 Early

The BDDI forms the foundation of my bass tone, offering a rich, thick, and warm sound.

I originally chose it to serve as a preamp for my passive bass.

Its Parallel Output is especially useful for capturing a separate dry signal.

I also prefer the rougher, wilder character of the older V1 over the V2.

One downside is its noticeably scooped midrange.

Origin EffectsCali76 Compact Deluxe

This pedal is an 1176-style FET compressor.
Although a bass-specific version is available, I also use the Compact Deluxe from my guitar pedalboard for bass.

I especially like its adjustable attack and release controls.
I use them to bring the bass slightly forward in the band mix when needed.

UAFX LA-2A

This pedal was the first effect pedal I bought for bass.

I had already enjoyed using the LA-2A plugin as a leveler and limiter.

It adds a smooth, rounded tube-like character and balances the overall volume, making the bass more comfortable to play.

However, excessive compression can make the sound muddy.

I use Comp mode when playing through an amplifier and Limit mode for direct PA setups or recording.

Spaceman Equinox

The Equinox is an analog pedal inspired by the Pultec EQP-1A, featuring the classic "Pultec Trick."

Unlike a typical parametric EQ, its frequency response changes are not always intuitive.
However, I love how it makes the sound deeper, tighter, and warmer.

Since I prefer subtle EQ adjustments over extreme changes, I mainly use it to gently refine my overall bass tone.

My Bass Guitars

Yamaha BB435

The BB435 is a 5-string bass with a PJ pickup configuration, featuring a Precision-style neck pickup and a Jazz-style bridge pickup.

I mainly favor the P pickup for a tight, full-bodied tone, using the J pickup to add brightness and sparkle. When I want a Jaco Pastorius-style tone, I use the J pickup alone.

I prefer a slightly darker overall tone, as the BB435 can sound a bit too bright for me.

I also use a body-through stringing setup, which gives me a sense of the strings' vibrations resonating more directly through the body.

Overall, it is a versatile, all-around bass that works well across a wide range of genres and songs.
