MIRACLE

Lower-Limb Exoskeleton   

Mechanical Design, Dynamic Modeling & Sensorless Impedance Control 

Overview

MIRACLE is a government-funded interdisciplinary research project focused on developing robotic systems to assist individuals with walking impairments.

MECHANICAL DEVELOPMENT

As a member of the Robotics Team at Yonsei University's MLCS Lab, I contributed to the mechanical development of a pediatric lower-limb exoskeleton, from CAD design to fabrication and assembly.

DYNAMICS & CONTROL

Building on the physical prototype, I modeled the exoskeleton's dynamics and investigated sensorless impedance control. I subsequently designed a disturbance-observer-enhanced control architecture and evaluated its tracking performance in MATLAB simulations.

Sensorless Impedance & Robust Control

From interaction
to robust control.

BASELINE

ROB-Based Sensorless Impedance Control 

For a wearable robot, following a predefined trajectory alone is insufficient. The robot must also respond to external forces generated through interaction with the wearer.

To address this, I implemented a sensorless impedance control framework based on a Reaction Torque Observer (ROB), following the approach proposed by Tran et al. Instead of relying on additional force/torque sensors, the ROB estimates external interaction torque using motor encoder feedback and the robot's dynamic model.

DESIGN MOTIVATION

Although this framework enables sensorless impedance control, its performance can be affected by uncertainties in the dynamic model.
During the development of the baseline controller, I observed a delay between the reference gait trajectory and the actual joint motion.
This motivated me to investigate an additional disturbance compensation method to improve tracking performance.

PROPOSED

Disturbance Observer-Enhanced Robust Control

To address this, I integrated a nonlinear Disturbance Observer (DO) into the existing ROB-based sensorless impedance control framework.

Unlike previous approaches that combine the two observers in parallel, I proposed a serial architecture in which the DO first estimates and compensates for disturbances and model uncertainties. The ROB then estimates the remaining external interaction torque using the compensated system dynamics.

My goal was to improve the accuracy of external torque estimation and reduce trajectory-tracking errors.

Observer Design & Stability Analysis
A. ROB-Based Sensorless Impedance Control

Based on the derived dynamic model, I formulated the external torque estimation process under the model compensation assumptions.
However, directly differentiating encoder measurements can amplify noise.
To address this, the ROB uses a low-pass filter to estimate external interaction torque while reducing the effects of measurement noise.

Equation 09 — Reaction Torque Estimation
u^ext=gs+g(ua−M^(q)sq˙)\hat{u}_{\mathrm{ext}} = \frac{g}{s+g}\left(u_a - \hat{M}(q)s\dot{q}\right)u^ext​=s+gg​(ua​−M^(q)sq˙​)

I then incorporated the estimated interaction torque into the desired impedance model.
By defining the desired inertia, damping, and stiffness, the controller can adjust the exoskeleton's mechanical response to external interaction.

Using the impedance tracking error, I derived the commanded acceleration and joint torque to achieve trajectory tracking while maintaining the desired impedance behavior.

B. Disturbance Observer Design

Disturbance Observer-Enhanced Robust Control

Starting from the previously derived equation of motion, I formulated the disturbance model and designed a nonlinear observer to estimate the disturbance torque.

To avoid directly using joint acceleration measurements, which can amplify encoder noise through numerical differentiation, I introduced an auxiliary vector.
This allowed me to formulate the observer using the robot's dynamic model and joint velocity information.

Equation 15 — Disturbance Model
d=M(q)q¨+C(q,q˙)q˙+G(q)−ud = M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q,\dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) - ud=M(q)q¨​+C(q,q˙​)q˙​+G(q)−u
Equation 16 — Auxiliary Vector
z=d^−p(q,q˙)z = \hat{d} - p(q,\dot{q})z=d^−p(q,q˙​)

I then designed the auxiliary function based on the joint velocities and derived the corresponding observer gain matrix using the inertia matrix.

Equation 17 — Auxiliary Function
p(q,q˙)=c[q˙1q˙1+q˙2]p(q,\dot{q}) = c\begin{bmatrix} \dot{q}_1 \\ \dot{q}_1 + \dot{q}_2 \end{bmatrix}p(q,q˙​)=c[q˙​1​q˙​1​+q˙​2​​]
Equation 18 — Observer Gain Matrix
L(q)=c[1011]M(q)−1L(q) = c\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}M(q)^{-1}L(q)=c[11​01​]M(q)−1

By substituting the auxiliary function into the observer formulation, I derived the observer dynamics without explicitly requiring joint acceleration measurements.

The estimated disturbance can then be reconstructed from the auxiliary vector and the designed function.
This estimate is used for disturbance compensation in the proposed controller.

Equation 19 — Disturbance Observer Dynamics
z˙=L(q)[C(q,q˙)q˙+G(q)−u−z−p(q,q˙)]\dot{z} = L(q)\bigl[C(q,\dot{q})\dot{q} + G(q) - u - z - p(q,\dot{q})\bigr]z˙=L(q)[C(q,q˙​)q˙​+G(q)−u−z−p(q,q˙​)]
Equation 20 — Estimated Disturbance
d^=z+p(q,q˙)\hat{d} = z + p(q,\dot{q})d^=z+p(q,q˙​)

To ensure that the disturbance observer could provide a reliable estimate, I further analyzed its estimation-error dynamics using Lyapunov theory.
This analysis allowed me to derive a sufficient condition for selecting the observer gain based on the mechanical parameters of the exoskeleton.

C. Stability Analysis

Lyapunov-Based Stability Analysis 

To examine the stability of the proposed disturbance observer, I first defined the disturbance estimation error as the difference between the actual and estimated disturbances.

Assuming that the disturbance remains approximately constant over the observer's estimation timescale, I derived the estimation-error dynamics.
This provided the foundation for analyzing whether the observer error converges to zero.

Equation 21 — Disturbance Estimation Error
e=d−d^e = d - \hat{d}e=d−d^
Equation 22 — Estimation Error Dynamics
e˙+L(q)e=0\dot{e} + L(q)e = 0e˙+L(q)e=0

To analyze the estimation-error dynamics, I introduced a coordinate transformation of the inertia matrix.
This allowed me to express the observer dynamics in a form suitable for Lyapunov analysis.

Using the transformed inertia matrix, I constructed a positive-definite Lyapunov candidate function to examine the convergence of the disturbance estimation error.

Equation 23 — Inertia Matrix Transformation
Mˉ(q)=ATM~(q)A,A=[1011]\bar{M}(q) = A^T\widetilde{M}(q)A,\qquad A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}Mˉ(q)=ATM(q)A,A=[11​01​]
Equation 24 — Lyapunov Candidate Function
V(e,q)=eTMˉ(q)eV(e,q) = e^T\bar{M}(q)eV(e,q)=eTMˉ(q)e

I then differentiated the Lyapunov function with respect to time and substituted the estimation-error dynamics.

By expressing its derivative as a quadratic form, I examined the conditions required for the derivative to remain negative definite.
This led to a sufficient observer gain condition based on the mechanical parameters and the maximum angular velocity of the second link.

Equation 25 — Lyapunov Derivative
V˙(e,q)=−eTQ(q,q˙)e\dot{V}(e,q) = -e^T Q(q,\dot{q})eV˙(e,q)=−eTQ(q,q˙​)e
Equation 26 — Positive-Definiteness Condition
Q(q,q˙)=[2c−c+η−c+η2c]>0Q(q,\dot{q}) = \begin{bmatrix} 2c & -c + \eta \\ -c + \eta & 2c \end{bmatrix} > 0Q(q,q˙​)=[2c−c+η​−c+η2c​]>0
η=m2l1lc2q˙2sin⁡(q2)\eta = m_2 l_1 l_{c2}\dot{q}_2\sin(q_2)η=m2​l1​lc2​q˙​2​sin(q2​)
Equation 27 — Observer Gain Condition
c>m2l1lc2q˙2,max⁡c > m_2 l_1 l_{c2}\dot{q}_{2,\max}c>m2​l1​lc2​q˙​2,max​

The resulting condition relates the observer gain to the mass, geometry, and maximum angular velocity of the second link.

Under the assumptions used in the analysis, satisfying this condition provides a sufficient basis for asymptotic convergence of the disturbance estimation error.

This analysis allowed me to establish a theoretical criterion for selecting the observer gain rather than relying solely on empirical tuning.
The proposed controller was subsequently evaluated through MATLAB simulations.

Results & Evaluation

MATLAB SIMULATIONS

Trajectory Tracking Performance

I evaluated the proposed control architecture in MATLAB by comparing the baseline ROB-only controller with the enhanced DO+ROB controller.

The animated plot compares the reference trajectory and the simulated joint response for the hip and knee joints. Compared with the ROB-only baseline, the DO+ROB controller followed the reference trajectory more closely, particularly at the knee joint.

This improvement is also reflected in the RMS tracking error. The enhanced controller reduced the RMS error by 14.7% at the hip joint and 27.4% at the knee joint, indicating improved trajectory-tracking performance in simulation.

Impedance Behavior under External Disturbances

To evaluate whether the proposed DO+ROB controller could maintain the desired impedance behavior under external disturbances, I conducted additional MATLAB simulations using two impedance settings: Soft and Stiff.

I applied simulated random disturbances and compared the joint responses under different damping and stiffness parameters while maintaining the same desired inertia. The Soft setting was designed to allow greater compliance, whereas the Stiff setting was intended to provide greater resistance to external disturbances.

The results showed that the Soft setting allowed larger deviations from the reference trajectories, while the Stiff setting maintained closer tracking, particularly at the hip joint. This behavior was consistent with the expected relationship between mechanical impedance and disturbance response.

The RMS tracking errors further confirmed these differences. Overall, the simulations demonstrated that the proposed DO+ROB controller retained the expected impedance-dependent behavior under external disturbances.

Evaluation Summary

Through simulations, I demonstrated that integrating the DO with the ROB-based controller reduced trajectory-tracking errors while maintaining the expected impedance behavior.

Although the enhanced controller has not yet been experimentally validated on the physical prototype, these results provide a basis for future implementation and evaluation on the MIRACLE exoskeleton.

My Contribution

What I
contributed.

Mechanical Design & Hardware Development

I was responsible for the mechanical design of the lower-limb exoskeleton, excluding the walker frame. I developed the CAD models of its structural and joint components and collaborated with manufacturing partners to fabricate and assemble the physical prototype.

Dynamic Modeling & Sensorless Control

Using the CAD-derived physical parameters, I formulated the two-link dynamic model through the Euler–Lagrange method. Based on previous research, I also developed and evaluated a ROB-based sensorless impedance controller.

Robust Control & Stability Analysis

To address the observed trajectory-tracking delay, I proposed a serial DO–ROB control architecture and derived the corresponding disturbance observer equations. I analyzed the observer's estimation-error convergence using Lyapunov theory and evaluated the proposed controller through MATLAB simulations, achieving 14.7% and 27.4% reductions in RMS tracking error at the hip and knee joints, respectively.
